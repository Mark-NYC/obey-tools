The question beneath the question

Hypocrisy is not a minor public-relations problem. Christians have sometimes hurt people, protected wrongdoing, or used religious language to justify harshness. Those experiences deserve to be heard and taken seriously, not explained away with a clever argument.

The life and teaching of Jesus include some of the Bible's strongest criticisms of religious hypocrisy. Whether Christianity is true is a different question from whether its followers always live up to it. Yet the gap between teaching and conduct still matters: it should lead Christians toward confession, accountability, restitution, and change.

Three questions worth discussing

  2. What would genuine accountability or repair look like after religious harm?
  3. How can someone evaluate Jesus fairly without ignoring the behavior of his followers?
