01 / A personal note

It starts at home.

Andrew and Philip turned eight this September, and it's been a joy watching their love for Jesus grow. When Meg substituted in their class at Flushing Christian School, she taught the students a simple way to share the gospel. Both boys jumped up to demonstrate and helped their classmates practice.

Later, Philip told her that he felt joy growing in his heart as he shared.

We've continued learning to trust God as we balance ministry, work, and raising our family in New York City. A family retreat in the Adirondacks this spring gave us much-needed time to hike, climb, rest, and be together.

God is teaching us to abide in Him as we pour into others. Thank you for being part of that story.

“Mom, sharing the gospel isn't scary, it's normal!”Philip, age 8

That's the heart of what we long to see: following Jesus becoming a normal part of everyday life, in every generation.

02 / Disciples making disciples

Stories you can't capture in a spreadsheet.

The numbers matter, but people matter more. Here are a few of the ways we've seen ordinary disciples take ownership of the mission.

Four generations · Queens

From Jim to Talha to a new generation

Jim discipled Talha, a Muslim-background believer, who is now investing in Makai and Andrew. These young men are learning to reach their own friends and families. Disciples are beginning to make disciples.

Gen Z · Queens

Five people come to faith at a basketball court

Chris began faithfully sharing Jesus at a neighborhood basketball court. Five people came to faith, one was baptized, and young disciples are learning to reach their friends.

Workplace · Manhattan

A church takes shape at Macy's

After being trained, Fabiola began sharing Jesus with her coworkers. Women came to faith, were baptized, and began gathering as a simple church. The workplace became a place for discipleship and community.

An ordinary day · Queens

From gospel conversation to baptism

Mark met Alex at a park in Queens. The next day Alex received Christ, was baptized, and began learning to share Jesus with friends. His journey is a reminder that obedience doesn't have to wait.

Heart languages · NYC

Himalayan believers reaching their own

Meg's Nepali and Tibetan Conversation Corner is increasingly led by local believers. People are sharing the gospel, praying for healing, and worshiping in three languages. We're praying for a multiplying Tibetan church to emerge.

Citywide · NYC

Churches learning to work together

Simple churches across Queens, Brooklyn, and Manhattan are gathering for worship, training, and outreach. At one park gathering, a person received Christ and two people experienced healing through prayer.

04 / Beyond Queens

Equipping people in their own places.

Queens to Nepal

We traveled to Nepal to equip leaders from multiple nations in starting, strengthening, and sustaining disciple-making movements. Back in New York, we also hosted an immersion where leaders from other cities learned through hands-on ministry in everyday life.

Stronger together in Queens

We've built new partnerships with other Queens churches and are gathering quarterly to pray, encourage one another, and pursue the mission together.

CoVo Multipliers is growing

Through the H3X podcast with David Miller in Oklahoma City, our Substack community has grown beyond 2,000 subscribers, and 50 practitioners are connecting on WhatsApp.

That network is moving beyond content and conversation. An online multiplier church is connecting new disciples and gatherings in Cadiz, Kentucky; Pace and Pensacola, Florida; Bay Ridge, Brooklyn; and Manhattan, with other places beginning to engage.

We're deepening collaboration between New York and Oklahoma City, with a vision to help the gospel reach people across North America where they live, work, and play.

05 / Digital tools for ordinary disciples

Obey Tools is moving into a new season.

We've been building Obey Tools to help disciples remember people, track gospel conversations, follow through on discipleship, and practice simple, reproducible patterns of obedience.

It's available in 12 languages, and disciples are already using the app across those languages. In 2027, we're preparing to launch on the App Store and continue building the next important step: helping disciples form new churches and track that progress.

Tracking what matters

Our metro-area dashboard is beginning to capture spiritual conversations and discovery activity. A recent snapshot displays 82 “Spiritual” and 54 “Discovery” entries across 2 cities.

These are the dashboard's current category labels, not a verified count of unique people, conversions, or churches. We'll share more detailed reporting as definitions and reporting periods are finalized.
